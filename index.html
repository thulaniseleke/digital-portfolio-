Tuckman's model identifies five stages of group development: Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing and Adjourning. The stages offer a useful framework for understanding how a team develops while working towards a shared goal.

Forming: At the beginning, the group needed to understand the assignment and get to know how members would work together. [Explain how the group was formed and how tasks were first discussed.]

Storming: Teams can face different opinions, scheduling problems or uncertainty about responsibilities. [Describe a real challenge, or explain if your group had few disagreements, and how it handled the situation.]

Norming: As work progressed, shared expectations about communication, deadlines and task allocation became important. [Describe the routines or agreements your group actually used.]

Performing: At this stage, team members work together towards completing the task. [Explain how your group coordinated the work and what helped or hindered progress.]

Adjourning: The final stage involves closing the task and reflecting on the experience. [Describe the submission or presentation, the outcome and any lessons discussed.]

It requires listening, clear communication, accountability and respect for different contributions.
