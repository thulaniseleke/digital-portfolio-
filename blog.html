Reflection 01 · Academic development

My Learning Journey: Building a Foundation for the Future

Beginning tertiary education is an important step in my personal and academic development. As a first-year Business Intelligence and Data Analytics student at Botswana Accountancy College, I am learning to take greater responsibility for my education and to consider how the knowledge I gain can be applied beyond the classroom.

One area I want to keep improving is time management. Tertiary education requires students to balance lectures, assignments, independent study and personal responsibilities. Planning ahead, dividing larger tasks into manageable steps and starting work early can help me reduce unnecessary pressure and produce better-quality work.

My interest in mathematics and computing gives me a useful foundation for this programme. I am particularly interested in how data can be organised, analysed and communicated to support informed decisions. Developing in this field will require consistent practice, attention to detail and a willingness to learn new tools. I want to strengthen my technical confidence while also learning to explain information clearly.

Communication and collaboration are also important parts of learning. Working with other students exposes me to different ideas and approaches. Listening carefully, asking questions when something is unclear and contributing responsibly to group tasks are habits I want to maintain.

Going forward, I intend to set realistic study goals, review class material regularly and seek clarification when necessary. I also want to use coursework and personal projects to practise relevant digital skills. Challenges are part of learning; the important thing is to identify what needs improvement and take practical steps to address it.

For me, this journey is about more than completing assessments. It is about becoming a disciplined, adaptable and responsible person who can contribute positively in a professional environment. By reflecting on my progress and applying what I learn, I aim to build the knowledge and confidence needed for future opportunities in data analytics.

A key goal for my next stage of learning is to connect classroom knowledge with practical work and keep building my confidence with analytical tools.

Reflection 02 · Collaboration

Understanding Group Work Through Tuckman's Model

Group work provides an opportunity to practise communication, shared responsibility and problem-solving. Tuckman's model describes five stages of group development: Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing and Adjourning. These stages provide a framework for reflecting on how a team develops while working towards a shared objective.

01. Forming

At the beginning of a group task, members become familiar with one another and try to understand the requirements of the work. Clear instructions and early discussion can help everyone understand the shared objective.

[Add how your group was formed and how you first divided the work.]

02. Storming

Different opinions, schedules and expectations can create difficulties. Listening to one another and discussing concerns respectfully can help a group work through these challenges.

[Describe a real challenge your group faced, or explain how you avoided major disagreements.]

03. Norming

As a team becomes more organised, members develop shared expectations and ways of communicating. Agreeing on deadlines and responsibilities can improve coordination.

[Explain the arrangements or routines your group used.]

04. Performing

At the performing stage, members coordinate their contributions and focus on completing the task. Progress depends on each person understanding and fulfilling their responsibilities.

[Describe how your group completed the work and what you contributed.]

05. Adjourning

The final stage involves completing the task and reflecting on what the team achieved and what could be improved in future.

[Explain the outcome of your project and one lesson you learned.]

This model helps show that successful teamwork involves more than dividing tasks. It also requires accountability, communication, respect and a willingness to adapt. In future group projects, I want to clarify expectations early, contribute consistently and communicate openly when challenges arise.

My main takeaway is that effective teamwork depends on both individual responsibility and the way team members support the group's shared goal.
